# Notes on arXiv version 5

These observations accompany the autoformalization of Metzger and Ulrigg’s *An Efficient Genus Algorithm Based on Graph Rotations*. The first two observations have explicit examples in Lean. The final three concern the interpretation of the algorithm and its analysis. [Paper, version 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.07347v5)

**Proposition 2.** Proposition 1(2) excludes opposite turns in distinct trails. The proof of Proposition 2 also uses this exclusion when the two turns occur in a single trail. On the four-spoked wheel, two nonbacktracking trails satisfy the printed conditions but induce a pair of transpositions at the hub. The strengthened rule excludes opposite turns at a vertex of degree greater than two wherever they occur. In a complete dart cover, the turns at each vertex define a permutation. Nonbacktracking excludes fixed points and the strengthened rule excludes 2-cycles at degree greater than two. Two remaining cycles would need at least six incident edges. At degree two the unique cyclic order is a transposition. [Wheel example](Page/PaperWitnesses.lean) · [Lean local tests](Page/HoGLocal.lean)

**Section 6.3.** The reverse dart must occur exactly once. It may occur in the same face. An explicit embedding of $K_{3,3}$ on the torus puts both darts of edge 03 in one face. Only a still-uncovered dart must occur in a subsequent selected face. [Lean example](Page/PaperWitnesses.lean)

**Bridges and degree-two vertices.** Suppressing vertices of degree two need not remove all bridges. The audited materialized program splits a graph with bridges into its biconnected blocks. Genus additivity applies to that decomposition. On the bridgeless face-length examples it retains the degree-two vertices. Suppressing them recovers the smaller cubic graph. [Audited source](upstream/genus.c) · [Face-length examples](audit/FaceCap/README.md) · [Battle, Harary, Kodama, and Youngs, *Additivity of the genus of a graph*](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9904-1962-10847-7)

**Search order.** Algorithm 1 returns the first full fit it finds. Taking the first successful target yields optimality when the outer loop tests the target face counts in decreasing order. In §7.2 a least vertex may occur several times on one closed trail, so starting at a least vertex can produce several cyclic representatives. The Lean search uses a least dart to select a representative. [Canonical representatives](Page/Canonical.lean)

**Theorem 8.** Counting subsets of darts omits the order in which a trail traverses them. To see the effect, take a wheel whose rim vertices are $a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_k,b_k$ in order. Write $v$ for the hub and use the $k$ directed triangles $v,a_i,b_i,v$. Concatenating the triangles in each cyclic order gives $(k-1)!$ distinct nonbacktracking directed closed trails on the same set of darts. The graph has $m=4k$ edges, so this count eventually exceeds any fixed constant times $4^m$. This addresses the intermediate trail count in the paper; the final running-time bound would need a separate argument.
